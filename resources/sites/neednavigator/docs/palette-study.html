Give the green a dark mass and the logo a home

Your portal screenshot settled this, so I rebuilt the study around it. The marketing site's hues were never the problem. What it is missing is a ramp and a dark mass: green exists only as a hairline accent and a single deep tone used once, on the closing band, so the page reads as beige with a green trim and the white wordmark has nowhere to stand.

I pulled the portal's stylesheet. It ships the marketing site's tokens verbatim, then adds the six-stop green ramp and the brass the site never had, and spends the dark end of that ramp on a full-height rail. That is the whole difference between the two.

So the first direction below is not a new palette. It is the site's own palette, extended to match the portal, which buys product-to-marketing continuity for free. Two more adventurous directions follow. Every specimen uses the site's real components and its two real typefaces, so the only variable is colour.

What I found while checking this

Is it as simple as flipping the top nav? Almost

Flipping the nav band alone gets you most of it and costs about a dozen lines. The one thing to decide is what sits directly under it. Right now that is cream, so a dark nav on its own reads as a stripe laid over a light page rather than as the top of the page.

Nav only works if the band is given weight: keep it full-bleed, let it run a little taller than it does today, and finish it with a harvest hairline instead of the current grey one, so it reads as a masthead rather than a floating bar. Cheapest option, and reversible in an afternoon.

Nav plus hero, which is what the specimen above shows, is the version that actually looks like your portal. The dark mass runs from the top of the page through the headline, then hands off to cream for the body. It is more work only because the hero has never had a dark variant, and it is the one I would build.

Either way the demo button goes white, which you already called. White on the mossy green measures 12.9 to one, so it is the strongest thing in the band, which is correct for the one action you want people to take.

The portal is already running your marketing tokens

I pulled the portal's compiled stylesheet rather than guess at the screenshot. Its paper, surface, card, wash and deep evergreen are the marketing site's values to the digit: #faf8f3, #f1ede2, #fffdf9, #e9efe8, #14382a. What it adds is the middle and the ends of the ramp, #0e281e, #3f6f56, #a9bfab, #cfdccf, plus a brass run from #e4bd80 down to #854d0e.

So the two properties are not in different palettes. The site has fewer stops and never uses the dark ones for area. That is worth saying plainly because it makes Portal Green cheap: the direction is additive, and nothing already on the page has to change hue.

Red as the action colour costs you the denial colour

This is the argument against Hull and Ember. Need Navigator's own screens lean on red to mean stop: a denied assistance request, a blocked service limit, a validation error. Promote red to the button colour and every stylised screenshot has to find a second red for refusal, which is why its chips above fall back to oxblood and a green appears for approval in a palette that otherwise has no green. Portal Green keeps red scarce, so red still means what it means.

One thing I got wrong on the first pass

I had flagged that the brand red measures poorly against a dark ground, three to one on near-black and only 2.2 to one on the evergreen. That figure is real but it does not apply to the logo, because the red arms of the life ring sit inside a white disc and never touch the ground. I composited the wordmark on all four candidate grounds to confirm it, and the mark holds on every one.

The constraint is narrower than I first wrote: red text or hairlines on the dark ground would fail, so keep red off the dark surfaces except as a shape with its own light backing. On paper it is fine at 5.5 to one.

The logo file itself is clipped, and it is the same file you already had

The file you dropped in the working tree is byte for byte the one committed in July as the site's white logo. Both end mid stroke: the final R is cut off by the right edge of the canvas, at 250 by 29 pixels with no margin. It reads as a rendering fault at any size, and at 21 pixels tall in a header it will not survive a retina screen either.

The dark version in the corner today is a raster recolour of that same white file. Inverting a white wordmark filled the life ring's negative space with black, which is what makes it look wrong. A dark ground removes the need for that file entirely. A traced vector wordmark would fix the clipping and the resolution together, and the image manifest already has a slot open for exactly that.

Either direction is a token edit, not a rebuild

The stylesheet is already fully tokenised, and every icon inherits currentColor. For Portal Green nothing needs a new hue at all, so the work is structural rather than chromatic.

  • Add the missing ramp stops and the brass run to :root, copied from the portal so the two stay in step.
  • Give the header, nav and hero a dark variant on --evergreen-deep. This is the only genuinely new component work, since neither has ever had one.
  • Serve the original white logo everywhere and retire the recoloured file.
  • Spend brass where cream is currently doing the warmth alone: eyebrows, icon tiles, the active nav underline.
  • Re-check the stylised screenshots, which hand-set a few colours rather than reading tokens.
Specimens use the site's own faces, Source Serif 4 and Public Sans, and its real components: nav, hero, funding pool meter and request status chips. Contrast figures are computed against WCAG 2.1, small text unless noted.
